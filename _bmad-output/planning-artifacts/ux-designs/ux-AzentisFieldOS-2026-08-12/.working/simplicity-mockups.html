Mockups: what the supervisor-first app would look like

Every screen below is a proposal rendered with the product's real design tokens and realistic site data — nothing here has been built. Sections marked Current show the app as it is today; Proposed shows the change. Where there is a genuine layout choice, both options are shown. Decisions you need to make are numbered D1–D6 and collected at the end.

Status: all implementation is paused. The code written earlier today is parked in a git stash, untouched — none of it is in the app. Nothing proceeds until you pick from this page.

Section 1 · Supervisor Home

What a supervisor sees when they open the app

Today a supervisor lands on the Owner's cross-site money dashboard, and every daily task is 3+ taps behind the hamburger. Proposal: supervisors get a task-first Home — report status on top, six big buttons, one tap each. The Owner's dashboard is unchanged.

Decision D1 — pick layout 1A (grid) or 1B (list)

Section 2 · Supervisor Navigation

The menu drawer, trimmed for supervisors

Today a supervisor's menu shows all 13 owner items (Vendors, Payments, Reports…). Proposal: supervisors see 7 items covering their real work. Nothing is removed from the product — owner surfaces stay reachable from Home → More and by direct link, and the Owner's own menu is untouched.

Decision D2 — approve trim, or adjust which items stay

Section 3 · Wide tables on phones

Payments, RMC, Expenses, Movements & Waste as cards on mobile

These lists have 7–9 columns; on a phone the amount and the Correct button fall off-screen and must be found by sideways swiping. Proposal: below tablet width, each row becomes a card showing the key facts, with the action always visible. Desktop keeps the exact table it has today.

Decision D3 — yes to cards, or keep sideways-scrolling tables

Section 4 · Corrections

Fixing a wrong quantity without doing maths

Scenario: yesterday's cement purchase from Shree Balaji Traders was recorded as 100 bags; it was actually 80. Today the user must calculate and type -20 themselves. Proposal: they type the correct number, 80, and the app shows and records the difference. The permanent ledger underneath is completely unchanged — it still stores a linked, reason-carrying adjustment entry.

Decision D4 — corrected-value entry, or keep delta with a live preview

Section 5 · Entry forms

Record Purchase: 15 fields → 7 visible, total auto-computed

Today the purchase form shows 15 fields at once and makes the user hand-type both Rate and Total Amount (a mismatch is easy and matters — it's money). Proposal: optional paperwork fields fold into "More details", the Total computes itself from quantity × rate (still editable if the bill differs), and mistakes show up while typing instead of after pressing Submit.

Decision D5 — approve all three (collapse + auto-total + inline validation), or pick a subset

Section 6 · One name per thing

Terminology: four names for the daily report become one

The same report is called "Daily Activity" in the menu, "Daily Site Report" in page text, "DSR" in badges and columns, and "New Report" on the button. Proposal: "Daily Report" everywhere a user reads it. Trade vocabulary the field already uses — Godown, Challan, Advance — stays exactly as-is; only our own inconsistencies change. Page addresses don't change, so bookmarks keep working.

Decision D6 — approve "Daily Report", or choose a different single name

Section 7 · The web app on desktop

Same changes in the browser — and what stays untouched

Everything above is one web app, not a separate mobile app. On a laptop the supervisor gets the same Home with the trimmed sidebar always visible (no bottom bar needed — the rail does that job). The Owner's desktop dashboard keeps everything it has today; its only change is one new button. Desktop tables stay tables (cards are phones-only), and the simplified forms work identically on both.

No new decision — covered by D1, D2 and D5

Also true on desktop: the simplified Purchase form (Section 5) and corrected-value corrections (Section 4) behave exactly the same in the browser — same fields, more horizontal room. The Payments/RMC/Expenses tables keep their current full-column desktop layout; the card view (Section 3) applies only below tablet width.

Section 8 · New — Inward entries without prices (your request)

Supervisor records what arrived; the Owner prices it later

Requested change: when a supervisor records Material Received, they should not see the money section at all — no Rate, no Total Amount, no Payment Status. They record the physical facts (vendor, material, quantity, challan photo). The entry is saved immediately and stock goes up, but it sits as "Pricing pending" until the Owner opens it and enters rate and amount — with the quantity shown for verification. The Owner's own inward entries keep the full form with prices, exactly as in Section 5.

Decision D7 — approve this flow, or adjust which fields the supervisor sees

Notes on how this behaves: stock is updated the moment the supervisor saves — pricing never blocks the material being usable. Vendor Outstanding and expense figures simply exclude unpriced entries until the Owner completes them (shown honestly as "2 pending", never as ₹0). And the permanent-ledger rule is respected: the supervisor's entry is never overwritten — the Owner is filling in fields that were recorded as "to be priced", one time; any change after pricing goes through the normal Correct flow. This applies to the supervisor's direct vendor-to-site entries too.
