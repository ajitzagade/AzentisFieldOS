# PRD Quality Review — AzentisFieldOS

## Overall verdict
This is a strong draft: the Vision is earned and specific, trade-offs are surfaced honestly rather than smoothed over (§6 scope philosophy, §8 timeline/multi-tenant tension), and the assumption-tagging discipline is genuinely rigorous with a clean Assumptions Index roundtrip. The main risks are downstream, not conceptual: a handful of dashboard FRs (FR-34, FR-37) lack testable consequences and one term ("per-Site progress," FR-34) is never defined; role/permission granularity is unresolved and touches ~6 FRs' acceptance criteria; and a few cross-references point at the wrong section (§6 vs §8). None of these are conceptual flaws — they're specific, fixable gaps in an otherwise decision-ready document.

## Decision-readiness — strong
Trade-offs are named, not dodged. §6's "Note on scope philosophy" states the founder "explicitly rejected a phased/stripped MVP" and owns the consequence ("accepting that it will be tested and refined against live use"). §8's "Timeline and execution risk" is unusually candid: "This PRD does not resolve that tension — it's named here so Architecture and Epics/Stories sequencing treat it as a real constraint, not an incidental detail." The nine Open Questions (§10) are genuinely open, not rhetorical — none answer themselves in the next sentence. The "never automatic" advance-adjustment decision (§4.6) is stated as a real decision with its own compensating control (append-only audit, FR-54) rather than hedged.

### Findings
- **low** No `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts used anywhere, despite real tensions existing in prose (§6 scope-philosophy note, §8 timeline/execution-risk paragraph, §8 platform/offline tension). The substance is present and readable, but the convention the rest of the PRD uses consistently (`[ASSUMPTION]`, `[Founder-confirmed]`) is absent for PM-facing tensions specifically. *Fix:* retrofit `[NOTE FOR PM]` tags onto the §6 and §8 paragraphs that already carry this content, so downstream tooling/skimming can find them.

## Substance over theater — strong
Three personas (Ramesh, Suresh, the founder), each driving specific FRs (UJ-1→FR-28/29, UJ-2→FR-32/33/35, UJ-3→FR-22–25, UJ-4→FR-52/53) — no persona theater. The Vision (§1) is not swappable: it names the pilot's actual scale ("10+ concurrently and roughly 70+ over a year"), and its competitive claim is backed by the addendum's Competitive Landscape Digest (Fieldwire narrow, RAKEN no inventory, Powerplay "generic rather than tuned"), not asserted for free. NFRs are mostly non-boilerplate: NFR-1 specifies "under any input including malformed or adversarial requests"; NFR-6 and NFR-2 are honestly flagged as unset rather than papered over with "reasonable" language.

### Findings
- **low** NFR-7 ("API-driven + Modular + Configurable + Scalable + Multi-tenant") is close to boilerplate phrasing, though it's tied to a direct founder quote and given a testable consequence (new modules addable without rewriting; config surfaces need no code deployment). Borderline but earns its place — flagged for awareness, not as a defect.

## Strategic coherence — strong
The thesis is explicit and singular: replace paper-book tracking with daily visibility ("a contractor owner opens the app and knows, for every site, what happened today — without phoning a supervisor," §1). The DSR is repeatedly and consistently named the load-bearing feature ("the single most important daily workflow," §4.8). Success metrics validate the thesis rather than measuring generic activity — SM-1 (DSR submission rate), SM-2 (time-to-submit), SM-3 (qualitative "no longer need to phone supervisors") all trace to the daily-visibility bet, not a DAU/MAU substitute. Counter-metrics are real and specific: SM-C1 warns against rushed/inaccurate DSRs to hit SM-1, SM-C2 warns against fabricated stock figures for SM-5 — neither is boilerplate "don't game the metric."

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Most FRs (roughly 45 of 54) carry an explicit "Consequences (testable)" block with concrete, verifiable conditions (e.g., FR-14's reconciliation identity, FR-52's "verified by attempting cross-tenant access and confirming it is rejected, not merely hidden in the UI"). This is unusually rigorous for a fast-path draft. But it isn't uniform, and the gaps sit in a load-bearing section (the Dashboard, which realizes UJ-2).

### Findings
- **medium** FR-34 ("Dashboard shows total/active/completed Sites and **per-Site progress** at a glance") never defines what "progress" is computed from. No FR anywhere establishes a % complete, milestone, or schedule-based measure — FR-1's Site fields only include status (active/completed/on-hold), not a progress metric. An engineer cannot build this tile as specified. *Fix:* either define a progress calculation (e.g., derived from planned vs. completed work items in DSRs) or scope FR-34 down to status-only and drop "progress" until defined.
- **medium** FR-34 and FR-37 have no "Consequences (testable)" block at all, unlike their siblings FR-35/FR-36/FR-38 in the same §4.10 Dashboard section. FR-38's consequence ("Every summary tile supports drill-down...") reads like it may be intended to cover the whole section (compare the explicit "applies to FR-42 through FR-46" pattern used in §4.13), but it isn't scoped that way, so it's ambiguous whether it applies to FR-34/FR-37 too. *Fix:* either give FR-34/FR-37 their own Consequences, or make FR-38's block explicitly say "applies to FR-34 through FR-38" as §4.13 and §4.14 do for their sections.
- **medium** Role/permission granularity is unresolved (Glossary §3 Role entry, Open Question 6) and it cascades into done-ness ambiguity across at least five FRs that hedge with "Owner/Admin or Site Supervisor" without a resolution rule: FR-10 (direct Vendor→Site purchase), FR-12 (Consumption), FR-13 (Return/Wastage), FR-26 (RMC delivery), FR-41 (Expense). This is honestly flagged (Open Question 6, Assumptions Index), so it isn't scope dishonesty — but it means "done" for permission enforcement on roughly a tenth of the FR set can't be pinned down yet. *Fix:* resolve Open Question 6 before Architecture; consider whether it needs to become its own FR (a permission matrix) rather than a per-FR aside.

## Scope honesty — strong
§5 Non-Goals is substantive (10 explicit exclusions, each with a one-line reason) and cross-referenced from §6.2 rather than restated. `[ASSUMPTION]` tags are used precisely — 16 inline instances, all correctly indexed in §11 with no orphans in either direction (see Mechanical notes). The de-scoping is done openly: e.g. FR-7's Out of Scope note on cross-tenant catalog sharing, or §5's explicit "(All of the above are named in the founder's own 'future modules' list ... not rejected ideas)," which distinguishes "not now" from "no."

### Findings
- **medium** The nine Open Questions (§10) are not triaged by how blocking they are. Some are genuinely low-stakes to leave open (OQ7 regional-language support), but OQ4 (government compliance — could imply *new* FRs, not just NFR tightening) and OQ6 (role granularity, see Done-ness finding above) are architecture-blocking and read at the same weight as lower-stakes items in a flat numbered list. *Fix:* tag OQ4 and OQ6 (at minimum) as blocking-before-architecture so they don't get triaged alongside OQ7/OQ9.

## Downstream usability — adequate
Glossary (§3, ~24 terms) is thorough and terms are used consistently across FRs (Tenant, Site, Stock, Advance, etc.). FR numbering (FR-1–FR-54) is contiguous with no gaps or duplicates; NFR-1–7 and SM-1–5/C1–2 are likewise clean. Most cross-references resolve correctly (e.g., UJ-4 → "FR-52, FR-53" is accurate; FR-3 → "§4.10 Dashboard" is accurate). But several section-pointer cross-references are wrong, which matters because this PRD explicitly feeds UX/Architecture/Stories workflows that will follow these pointers.

### Findings
- **medium** §5 Non-Goals, first bullet: "No native mobile app in v1. Responsive web only, per platform decision (**§6, Constraints**)." §6 is "MVP Scope," not Constraints — the actual Platform/native-app decision lives in §8 ("Constraints and Guardrails" → "Platform" subsection). *Fix:* change to "(§8, Constraints)."
- **medium** §6.2, second bullet: "Native app / installable PWA — explicit platform decision for v1 (**§6 below** has the NFR detail)." This is self-referential — the bullet is already inside §6, and the NFR/platform-tension detail actually lives in §8 (Platform subsection) and §7 (NFR-2). *Fix:* change to point at §8 (or §7), not §6.
- **low** §3 Glossary Role entry's assumption tag says "not confirmed, see §8" — but §8 doesn't discuss role granularity; the actual discussion is Open Question 6 in §10 (which FR-10 correctly cites as "§10 Open Question 6"). Minor inconsistency between two citations of the same open item. *Fix:* align both citations to §10 Open Question 6.

## Shape fit — strong
This is a multi-stakeholder B2B tool (owner, supervisor, platform operator) with meaningful mobile UX (offline DSR entry) and a chain-top position (§0: "feeds ... UX, Architecture, Epics & Stories"). Four UJs with named protagonists is proportionate — not over-formalized (this isn't a single-operator internal tool where UJs would be overhead) and not under-formalized (a consumer-grade product with zero UJs would be a red flag; this has exactly as many as there are real actor types). The white-label/multi-tenant framing is treated as an architectural precondition (§4.15) rather than a bolted-on feature, which matches a product whose business model depends on tenant resale. Regulatory uncertainty (government compliance) is flagged as a genuinely open question (OQ4) rather than forced into false certainty either direction — correct calibration for a domain that plausibly-but-not-confirmedly touches government audit requirements.

- **ID continuity: clean.** FR-1 through FR-54 contiguous, no gaps/duplicates. NFR-1–7, SM-1–5 + SM-C1–2, UJ-1–4 all contiguous.

UJ-4's protagonist is "the founder" — a role, not a personal name, unlike the other three. Minor inconsistency, low stakes since the founder is a real single individual in this document's context, not a composite.
